# Stats and records

Everything you log feeds the **Stats** tab. There is nothing to set up: finish a workout and
the numbers are there.

## The overview

Pick a window along the top (7 days, 30 days, 90 days, a year, or all time) and the tab
shows, for that period:

- **Cumulative volume**: every kilo you moved, as a running total, with the change against the
  previous period;
- **Sessions**, and your **average per week**;
- **Records** broken, and across how many exercises;
- **Time lifted**, and the average session length;
- **Muscles trained**: a front and back body map shaded by how many sets each muscle got, with
  the counts beside it.

## Personal records

The app checks every finished workout for records, so you never have to mark one yourself.
For each exercise it tracks:

| Record | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Heaviest** | The most weight lifted in one set. |
| **Most reps** | The most reps in one set. |
| **Most volume** | The most weight × reps in one session. |
| **Estimated 1RM** | The one-rep max your best set implies, by the Epley formula or, if you prefer, Brzycki (<kbd>Settings</kbd> › <kbd>1RM formula</kbd>). |
| **Longest distance** and **best pace** | For cardio exercises logged by distance and time. |

Warm-up sets never count. New records are called out on the workout summary, flagged in your
history, and listed in **Stats** › **Personal records**, which you can filter by type.

## One exercise, in depth

<Screen name="progression" alt="Bench press over 90 days: estimated 1RM rising from 84 to 101 kg, volume per session, and recent attempts." caption="Exercise progression for bench press." />

**Stats** › **Exercise progression** lets you pick any exercise you have logged and see its
estimated one-rep max and its volume per session charted over the window you choose, its
records, and a table of recent attempts with the top set of each.

Charts are drawn for everyone: TalkBack reads each one as a sentence ("Cumulative volume, Feb
to Apr. Latest 108,400 kg…") rather than point by point.

## The training calendar

<Screen name="calendar" alt="April 2026 with five training days shaded by volume." caption="Sessions › Calendar." />

**Sessions** › **Calendar** shows each month at a glance. Training days are shaded by that
day's volume against the month's heaviest, so rest days and big days both stand out. Tap a day
to see what you did.
