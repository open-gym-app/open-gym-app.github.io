# <AppName /> privacy policy

*Effective 25 November 2026. Also available in [français](pathname:///fr/privacy), [español](pathname:///es/privacy) and [português (Brasil)](pathname:///pt-BR/privacy).*

<AppName /> is a workout tracker for Android. It is free, open source, and made by volunteers.
This policy covers the <AppName /> app (<AndroidPackage />) as published on Google Play and built
from the source repository.

## The short version

- <AppName /> **collects nothing**. There is no account, no analytics, no crash reporting and no
  advertising.
- The app **cannot connect to the internet**. It does not request Android's `INTERNET`
  permission, so the system does not let it open a network connection.
- Everything you log stays **on your phone**, in the app's private storage.
- Your data leaves the phone only when **you** export it, to a place **you** choose.
- You can **delete everything** at any time from the app.

## What the app stores, and where

<AppName /> keeps the following on your device so that it can work as a training log:

| What | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Workouts | sessions, exercises, sets, weights, reps, distances, times, elevation, steps, effort (RPE), rest times, calories, your rating of the workout, heart rate (only if a file you import contains it), notes |
| Your exercise library and templates | custom exercises, notes on exercises, saved workout templates |
| Body data | body weight, body fat percentage, body measurements (waist, arms, and so on), progress photos |
| Progress | personal records, experience points, level, streaks, quests, achievements, collected items |
| Settings | units, theme, training goal, fitness level, weekly goal, rest timer, reminder days and time, bar and plates |

This is kept in the app's private database, settings files and photo folder, which Android
lets no other app read.

If you turn on **Weekly auto-backup** (Settings › Backup & restore), the app also writes a
backup file once a week into its own backup folder, the one **Export a backup** writes to. It
keeps the three most recent and deletes older ones. On Android 11 and later, which keeps
other apps out of it, that folder is on the device's shared storage
(<AndroidPackage prefix="Android/data/" suffix="/files/backups" />). On Android 8 to 10, where other apps with
storage access could read that location, the folder is in the app's private internal storage
instead, and a backup leaves it only when you use **Share** or **Save a copy…** in
Settings › Backup & restore. These files stay on the phone, and Android deletes them when you
uninstall the app.

### Progress photos

Progress photos are optional. **Take a photo** opens your camera app, and **Choose from
gallery** opens Android's picker, which gives <AppName /> only the picture you choose. <AppName /> asks for
no camera or storage permission. It keeps its own copy of the picture in its private storage,
without the metadata a camera adds, such as where it was taken. Of that metadata it reads only
the date and which way up the picture is. A picture in your gallery is not changed, and your
camera app may keep a copy of its own.

Progress photos are never part of a backup, and the app has no way to send them anywhere. You
can delete each one in the app.

### Health data

Body weight, body fat, body measurements, progress photos, calories and heart rate are
health data. <AppName /> uses them for two purposes only: to show you your own entries and trends,
and, with your body weight, to estimate the calories a workout burned. <AppName /> does not
measure heart rate: it keeps it only if a file you import contains it, and only includes it
in your own backups, without showing it or using it for anything else. Health data is never
transmitted, sold, shared, or used for advertising. <AppName /> does not read from or write to
Health Connect, or any other health service. If a future version adds a Health Connect sync,
this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the sync will be off until
you turn it on.

## What the app does not do

- **No data collection.** Neither the <AppName /> project nor anyone else receives your data.
  We have no server that could receive it.
- **No network access.** The app does not request the `INTERNET` permission. It does
  request `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`, added by the Android job scheduler library it uses. That
  permission only lets it ask whether the phone is online; it cannot send or receive
  anything.
- **No third-party SDKs that collect data.** No analytics, crash reporting, advertising or
  tracking libraries are included. The full list of libraries is in the app, under
  Settings › About › Open source licences.
- **No cloud backup.** The app opts its data out of Android's automatic backup to Google
  Drive and of device-to-device transfer, so your training history and your photos are not
  copied off the phone behind your back.
- **No account and no advertising identifier.** The app does not know who you are.

## When your data leaves the phone

Only when you ask it to. **Export a backup** (Settings › Backup & restore) writes all of
your data, including your body data but not your progress photos, to a JSON file. You then
choose to save a copy somewhere or share it with another app. From that moment the copy is
handled by whatever you sent it to, under that service's own terms. The file is not
encrypted: keep it somewhere you trust.

**Restore** reads a backup file you pick and replaces the data on the phone with it, except
your progress photos, which it leaves alone. Nothing is uploaded.

**Import from another app** (Settings › Import from another app) reads a file you pick, which
you exported from another app or made yourself, and adds what it holds to your data. <AppName /> does
not sign in to the other app, does not change the file and uploads nothing. It keeps Android's
permission to read that one file until you close the result of the import, or start another
one, so that an interrupted import can be run again.

## Notifications

Workout reminders, the rest-timer alert, the unfinished-workout note, level and reward
notifications, and the progress of an import are all created on the phone. None is sent from a
server. You can turn each one off in Settings › Notifications or in Android's settings, except
the progress of an import, which is in Android's settings only.

## Permissions

| Permission | Why the app asks for it |
| --- | --- |
| `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` | To show the notifications above. Android 13+ asks you first. |
| `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` (Android 12 and below) / `USE_EXACT_ALARM` | To end a rest timer on the second, and to fire a workout reminder at the time you set. |
| `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED` | To schedule your workout reminders and the weekly backup again after the phone restarts. |
| `VIBRATE` | To buzz when a rest period ends. |
| `FOREGROUND_SERVICE`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_DATA_SYNC` | To keep an import from another app running, with a notification, while you do something else. "Data sync" is Android's name for this kind of work: neither grants network access, and nothing is sent. |
| `WAKE_LOCK`, `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE` | Added by Android's job scheduler library (WorkManager), which runs the weekly backup and the import. Neither grants network access. |
| <AndroidPackage suffix=".DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION" /> | Added by the AndroidX core library. It keeps other apps from sending the app internal messages. |

## Deleting your data

- **Settings › Delete all data** erases everything the app has stored, progress photos
  included, and resets every setting. It cannot be undone.
- **Uninstalling the app** deletes everything too, including the automatic backups and
  the progress photos.
- **Exported files** you saved or shared, and **files you imported**, are outside the app.
  Delete them wherever you put them.

Because we hold no copy of your data, there is nothing for us to delete, and no request to
send.
